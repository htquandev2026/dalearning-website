Vietnamese tones, explained simply

Vietnamese is a tonal language: the pitch of a syllable is part of the word. “ma” with a falling tone (mà) means “but”; with a rising tone (má) it means “cheek”, or “mom” in the South. The good news is that the spelling tells you the tone every time.

The six tones

  • ngang (no mark): ma, level and steady.
  • huyền (grave accent, à): mà, low and falling gently.
  • sắc (acute accent, á): má, rising.
  • hỏi (small hook, ả): mả, dipping down then up.
  • ngã (tilde, ã): mã, rising with a break in the voice.
  • nặng (dot below, ạ): mạ, short, low and heavy.

North and South

Hà Nội speech keeps all six tones apart. In Saigon and most of the South, hỏi and ngã sound almost the same, so you hear five. They are still written differently. Learn the accent your family or friends speak; both are correct.

How to practise

  • Listen to a pair, say it, record yourself, compare.
  • Practise whole phrases, not only single syllables: tones change slightly in real speech.
  • Ten minutes a day for a few weeks matters more than long sessions.
