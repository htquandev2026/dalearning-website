Heritage Vietnamese learners: you understand, now speak

Your parents spoke Vietnamese to you. You answered in English. Now you can follow most of a family dinner, but when an aunt asks you something directly, the words do not come. This is extremely common among children of Vietnamese families abroad, and it is a much better starting point than it feels like.

What you already have

  • An ear for the tones. You hear the difference between bà and bá even if you cannot explain it.
  • Your family’s accent, North, South or Central, already in your head.
  • Home vocabulary: food, family, feelings, the things you heard every day.
  • A reason to speak and people to speak with.

What usually holds people back

  • Shyness. Relatives laugh kindly at mistakes, and it still stings. Expect it, and keep going.
  • Pronouns. Not knowing whether to say con, cháu or em makes people go silent. Learn the system once: Vietnamese pronouns.
  • Reading. Many heritage speakers never learned the tone marks. Spelling is regular, so it takes weeks, not years: Vietnamese tones.
  • Vocabulary outside the home: work, news, feelings in detail.

How to start speaking

  1. Say out loud the phrases you already understand. Recognition turns into speech by repetition, not by study.
  2. Record yourself and compare with a model. Your ear is good; let it judge your voice. See pronunciation practice.
  3. Pick one relative and one routine, such as a weekly call with a parent, and speak only Vietnamese for the first five minutes.
  4. Tell the family what you are doing: “Con đang học lại tiếng Việt, bố mẹ sửa giúp con nhé.” Most will slow down and help.
  5. Keep the accent you grew up with, even if a course teaches a different one. Northern vs Southern.
