Vietnamese pronunciation practice that actually works

Reading about the six tones is useful once. After that, pronunciation improves only one way: hearing a model, saying it yourself, and finding out how close you were. Here is a routine that takes ten minutes a day.

The ten-minute routine

  1. Listen three times without speaking. Pay attention to whether the voice rises, falls, dips or stays level.
  2. Shadow: play it again and speak along with it, at the same time.
  3. Record yourself saying it alone.
  4. Compare. Play the model, then your recording. Better still, look at the pitch: is your line rising where the model rises?
  5. Repeat with the next phrase. Five phrases done well beat twenty skimmed.

Watch the pitch curve

Your ear is easily fooled by your own voice; a picture is not. In the Dạ app, the tone coach draws the rise and fall of your voice next to the model’s line, so you can see when your sắc tone is not rising high enough or your nặng tone is not short and low. After a few tries it also adjusts for how high or low your voice naturally is.

What to drill first

  • Minimal pairs, words that differ only by tone: bán / bạn, cá / cà, sửa / sữa. They are in the table below.
  • Tones in phrases, not only single words. “Con chào bà ạ” has four syllables and three different tones.
  • Final sounds: -c, -t, -p are stopped, not released. “Cát” ends abruptly.
  • Your accent’s sounds. In the South hỏi and ngã sound alike and “v” is often closer to “y”; see Northern vs Southern.
