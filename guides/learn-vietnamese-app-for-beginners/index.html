Choosing a Vietnamese app as a beginner

Vietnamese is not hard to start: the alphabet is Latin, there are no verb endings, and spelling follows sound. What makes it hard is listening and tones. A good beginner app should spend most of its time there.

What to look for

  • Audio for every phrase, and the choice of a Northern or Southern accent. Learning one when your family speaks the other is frustrating. See Northern vs Southern.
  • Speaking practice with feedback: record yourself and compare, ideally with a view of your pitch. See pronunciation practice.
  • The six tones taught early, with minimal pairs, not saved for later.
  • Phrases you will use with real people, not only single words.
  • Kinship words and pronouns. Without them you cannot greet anyone properly: Vietnamese pronouns.
  • A fair price model. Know whether it is a subscription or a single purchase before you invest your time.

Your first month

  1. Days 1–7: the tones and five greetings. Vietnamese tones.
  2. Days 8–14: the people around you: family words and how to address them.
  3. Days 15–21: one situation you will really be in, such as a family dinner, a phone call or ordering food.
  4. Days 22–30: use it. Three phrases on a real call, or at a restaurant.

Where Dạ fits

Dạ is built for beginners who are learning Vietnamese for people: grandparents, partners, in-laws, friends. It has three modes (Kids, Partner & family, Everyday), 200 topics in each, audio in a Northern or Southern accent, record-and-compare practice with a tone coach, and culture notes that explain why people say what they say. It works offline, has no ads and no accounts, and 50 topics in every mode are free. It is not a grammar course or exam preparation. If that is your goal, pair it with a textbook or a teacher.
