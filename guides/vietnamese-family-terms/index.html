Vietnamese family terms: what to call every relative

English has one word for aunt and one for uncle. Vietnamese has a different word depending on whose side of the family the person is on and whether they are older or younger than your parent. These words are not only names: they are also the pronouns you use when you talk to that person, and the word you use for yourself changes with them.

Father’s side and mother’s side

  • ông bà nội: your father’s parents. ông bà ngoại: your mother’s parents.
  • bác: an older sibling of your parent (in the North, on either side).
  • chú: your father’s younger brother. cô: your father’s sister.
  • cậu: your mother’s brother. dì: your mother’s sister.

Usage differs by region and by family. In the South, for example, “cô” and “dì” are often used for a father’s or mother’s sister whatever her age. When in doubt, ask: “Con gọi bác ấy là gì ạ?” (What should I call him or her?). Families are happy to explain.

How you call yourself

Talking to your parents’ generation you are “con” or “cháu”; to an older brother or sister you are “em”; to a younger one you are “anh” (if you are a man) or “chị” (if you are a woman). Getting this right matters more to most Vietnamese families than perfect tones. The full system, and why “tôi” sounds cold to a grandparent, is in Vietnamese pronouns.
