Vietnamese phrases for in-laws

In a Vietnamese family, your partner’s parents become your parents in the way you talk. You call them what your partner calls them, and you call yourself “con”, their child. That one change does more for the relationship than any amount of vocabulary.

What to call them

  • To their face: “bố mẹ” (North) or “ba má” (South), the same as your partner. Some families use “thầy u”, “cha mẹ” or “ba mẹ”; follow theirs.
  • When talking about them: “bố mẹ chồng” (husband’s parents), “bố mẹ vợ” (wife’s parents). Never to their face.
  • Yourself: “con”. Not “tôi”, which sounds like a stranger. See Vietnamese pronouns.
  • Your partner’s older siblings are “anh” and “chị” even if they are younger than you; their younger siblings are “em”. You take your partner’s place in the family order.

Saying thank you

Cảm ơn mẹ đã trông cháu.Cảm ơn má đã trông cháu.Thanks for looking after the kids, Mom.

Cuối tuần con mời bố mẹ đi ăn.Cuối tuần con mời ba má đi ăn.This weekend we’re taking you out to eat.
Có gì đâu con.(same)It’s nothing, dear.

Everyday politeness

  • Answer with “dạ” before anything else: “Dạ, con biết rồi ạ.”
  • Say where you are going: “Con xin phép ra ngoài một chút ạ.”
  • Offer to help in the kitchen and at the end of the meal. More in Vietnamese dinner phrases.
  • Call or message on their birthdays, at Tết and on death anniversaries (đám giỗ). See Tết greetings.

Asking a favour, politely
