Vietnamese phrases for a family dinner

Family meals are where you will use your Vietnamese most. Dishes are shared in the middle, everyone has a bowl of rice, and there is a small ritual before anyone starts.

Invite the elders first

Before eating, younger people invite the older ones: “Con mời bố mẹ ăn cơm.” If the table is big, “Con mời cả nhà ăn cơm ạ” covers everyone. Then wait for the oldest person to start.

“Ăn cơm chưa?”

Literally “Have you eaten rice yet?”, this is less a question about food than a way of saying “I care about you”. Grandparents and parents ask it on the phone, at the door and in the family group chat. Answer politely: “Dạ, con ăn rồi ạ” (Yes, I have eaten) or “Dạ, con chưa ạ” (Not yet). If you say not yet, expect to be fed. You can ask it back too: “Bà ăn cơm chưa ạ?”

Manners that matter

  • Take food from the shared dishes a little at a time.
  • Do not stand your chopsticks upright in the rice: it is linked to offerings for the dead.
  • Praise the cook: “Mẹ nấu ăn ngon quá!” is never wrong.
  • Expect to be offered more food. Saying “Con ăn no rồi ạ” politely tells them you are full.
  • Offer to help clear up: “Để con rửa bát cho ạ.”
