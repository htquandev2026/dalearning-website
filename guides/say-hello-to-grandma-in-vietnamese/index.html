How to say hello to your Vietnamese grandma

The phrase is “Con chào bà ạ.” It is usually the first full sentence a child learns, and the one that makes a Vietnamese grandmother smile every time.

Word by word

  • con: “I”, said by a child to an elder. It literally means “child”.
  • chào: to greet. On its own it works as “hello” or “bye”.
  • bà: grandma, and also “you” when you are talking to her.
  • ạ: a small word at the end that makes the sentence polite. Leaving it out to a grandparent sounds a little rude.

So the sentence is literally “Child greets grandma, politely.” There is no “I” or “you” in the English sense: you name yourself and her by your place in the family. Vietnamese pronouns explains why.

Con or cháu?

“Cháu” is the word for a grandchild, so “Cháu chào bà ạ” is also correct and is common with older children and adults. Many families, especially with small children, use “con” for grandchildren too. Use whatever your family uses; if you are not sure, listen to how your parents talk to bà.

Bà nội or bà ngoại?

Your father’s mother is bà nội; your mother’s mother is bà ngoại. You use these words when talking about her (“Bà nội con ở Huế”). Face to face, just “bà” is enough. To get her attention, call “Bà ơi!”. Grandpa works the same way: ông nội, ông ngoại, “Ông ơi!”. More in Vietnamese family terms.

On a video call

  • Start: “Con chào bà ạ! Bà có khoẻ không ạ?” (Hello grandma! Are you well?)
  • If she asks “Ăn cơm chưa?”, answer “Dạ, con ăn rồi ạ.” (Yes, I have eaten.)
  • Before you hang up: “Con yêu bà. Con chào bà ạ.” (I love you, grandma. Bye.)
  • Bow your head a little as you greet her, even on screen. Grandparents notice.
