Learn Vietnamese for kids: words to say to bà and ông

Many children of Vietnamese families understand some Vietnamese but answer in English. That is normal. Understanding comes first; speaking comes when a child has a few short phrases they feel safe using, and a person they want to use them with. For most kids that person is bà (grandma) or ông (grandpa).

Start with “Dạ”

“Dạ” is the polite way a child answers a grown-up: when grandma calls, the answer is “Dạ!”. It is one syllable, it makes grandparents smile, and it teaches the idea of politeness to elders that runs through the whole language. Add “ạ” at the end of a sentence and it becomes polite too: “Con cảm ơn ạ.” The first greeting, word by word, is in how to say hello to grandma in Vietnamese.

First phrases for grandparents

VietnameseEnglish
Dạ!Yes! (polite answer to grown-ups)
Con chào bà ạ.Hello grandma.
Con cảm ơn ạ.Thank you.
Con yêu bà.I love you, grandma.
Tạm biệt ạ.Goodbye.
Con xin lỗi ạ.I’m sorry.
Con mời ông bà ăn cơm ạ.Grandpa, grandma, please eat.
Con đi học đây ạ.I’m going to school now.
Con về rồi ạ.I’m home!
Chúc bà ngủ ngon.Good night, grandma.
Con dậy rồi ạ.I’m awake.
Con khoẻ ạ.I’m fine.

Make the video call the lesson

A weekly call with grandparents is the best practice there is. Pick two or three phrases before the call, say them together once, then let your child try them on the call. Keep it short and happy; a call that ends with a laugh is the one they will want to repeat.

Words for a video call

Habits that work at home

  • Use the same Vietnamese words for daily routines: meals, bath, bed.
  • Five minutes a day beats an hour on Saturday.
  • Praise the try, not the accent. Correct by repeating the phrase back the right way, not by saying “wrong”.
  • Let your child hear the accent your family speaks. If grandma is from Saigon, learn Southern words; if she is from Hà Nội, Northern (how to choose).
  • Young children do not need to read. Pictures and sound are enough.
