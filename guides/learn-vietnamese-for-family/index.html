Learn Vietnamese for family: where to start

Most people learning Vietnamese outside Vietnam are not learning it for an exam. They want to talk to someone: a grandmother on a video call, a partner’s parents at Tết, aunts and uncles at a wedding. That changes what you should learn first.

Family Vietnamese is its own thing

  • Kinship words come first. Vietnamese has a different word for each relative, and that word is also how you say “you”. See Vietnamese family terms.
  • There is no neutral “I”. With family you call yourself con, cháu or em, never “tôi”. See Vietnamese pronouns.
  • Politeness to elders is built into the words: “dạ” to answer, “ạ” at the end of a sentence.
  • The accent matters. Learn the one your family speaks: Northern vs Southern Vietnamese.

A four-week plan

  1. Week 1: greetings for the people you will actually see. Start with how to say hello to grandma.
  2. Week 2: the six tones, ten minutes a day. Vietnamese tones explained.
  3. Week 3: the family table. Phrases for a family dinner, including “Ăn cơm chưa?”.
  4. Week 4: one real conversation. Pick three phrases, use them on a call, and ask the family to correct you.

Married or partnered into a Vietnamese family? Start with meeting your partner’s parents and phrases for in-laws. Grew up hearing Vietnamese but never speaking it? Read the heritage learner guide. Teaching a child? See Vietnamese for kids.

What helps most

  • Short daily practice. Fifteen minutes every day beats two hours on Sunday.
  • Speak out loud from day one, and record yourself.
  • Use the family. Ask “Con nói đúng không ạ?” (Did I say that right?). Most relatives are delighted to help.
  • Learn whole phrases, not lists of words. A phrase you can say today is worth more than fifty words you can only recognise.
