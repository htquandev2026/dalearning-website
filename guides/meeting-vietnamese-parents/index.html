Meeting your Vietnamese partner’s parents

You do not need fluent Vietnamese to make a good first impression. A handful of polite phrases, said with the right words for each person, tells the family that you take them seriously. Most parents remember the effort far longer than the mistakes.

Three words that do most of the work

  • “Con”: what you call yourself when talking to your partner’s parents, the way their own child would.
  • “Dạ”: the polite yes. Use it whenever an elder speaks to you.
  • “ạ”: put it at the end of a sentence to an elder to make it polite.

Greetings

VietnameseEnglish
Dạ.Yes. (polite reply to an elder)
Con chào bố mẹ ạ.Hello Mom and Dad. (to your partner’s parents)
Con cảm ơn ạ.Thank you. (to an elder)
Anh có khoẻ không?Are you well? (to your older partner)
Rất vui được gặp bác ạ.Very nice to meet you. (to an older relative)
Con xin phép về ạ.May I take my leave? (goodbye to elders)
Em chào anh.Hi. (to an older man, e.g. your partner’s brother)
Em chào chị.Hi. (to an older woman, e.g. your partner’s sister)
Cháu chào bác ạ.Hello. (to an aunt or uncle older than your partner’s parents)
Cháu chào cô ạ.Hello. (to your partner’s aunt)

On the day

  • Greet the oldest person first, then the parents, then everyone else.
  • Bring a small gift (fruit, tea, something from your country) and offer it with both hands.
  • Wait to be invited to sit, or ask: “Con xin phép ngồi ạ.”
  • At the meal, invite the elders to eat before you start (see Vietnamese dinner phrases).

Ask your partner which accent and which words the family uses for parents: “bố mẹ” is more Northern, “ba má” more Southern. For the words you will use every week after the first visit, see Vietnamese phrases for in-laws.
