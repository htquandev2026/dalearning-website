Vietnamese New Year greetings: what to say at Tết

Tết, the Lunar New Year, is the biggest family event of the Vietnamese year. Visiting relatives and wishing them a good year is the heart of it, and a few well-chosen wishes in Vietnamese go a long way.

The basics

  • “Chúc mừng năm mới!”: Happy New Year. Works with anyone.
  • For elders, wish health and long life: “Con chúc ông bà sống lâu trăm tuổi ạ.”
  • For adults, wish health, peace and success: “An khang thịnh vượng!”
  • Start your wish with “Con chúc…” (to parents and grandparents) or “Cháu chúc…” (to aunts and uncles). See Vietnamese family terms for who is who.

Lucky money

Children and unmarried young people receive lucky money in red envelopes: “lì xì” in the South, “mừng tuổi” in the North. Receive it with both hands and say thank you. Once you are married or working, you may be the one giving.

Good to know

  • The first visitor of the year is thought to bring the household’s luck, so families often choose who comes first.
  • Avoid arguing, sweeping and talking about bad luck on the first days.
  • Families often visit the father’s side first, then the mother’s.
